One snapshot, and a broken index

Two things bound what this can claim.

This is a single day. The Iles teardown had four snapshots, so it could report what he killed and when. Here there is only today — so everything ranks on how long an ad has already been running, which is real, and nothing reports kill rates, which are invisible from one pull.

Only live ads were pulled. Any survival percentage computed from this data reads 100% and means nothing. Tenure is the metric.

Foreplay's discovery index cannot be ranked, and this is worth knowing before you trust any tool that uses it. Sorting it by longest-running returns running_duration: null on every row. Filtering it by a minimum duration returns ads that all report exactly the filter's floor, from an identical start date — the constraint echoed back as data. Its "service business" niche tag returns zero brands, and "business/professional" is a B2B-SaaS bucket holding Rocket Lawyer, Tines and Sticker Mule.

Every figure on this page comes from per-brand pulls instead, where tenure is genuine. Candidates were found by text search, which works; ranking came after.

The four openers worth stealing

Every one of them refuses the obvious pitch first

"Sacramento home service business owners, your lead problem isn't what you think it is. You don't need more leads…"

147 days · longest-running English opener in the set

"There are two types of med spa owners. The ones who get new patients predictably and on-demand, and those at the mercy of…"

121 days · forces self-sorting in one sentence

"Med spas, you don't have to run discount promos or limited-time specials to get new patients."

121 days · attacks the tactic, not the person

"If you're having a slower month, the first thing to check is…"

72-day median across 10 ads · conditional entry

Geo-naming, self-sorting, denying the expected offer, gating on a condition. None of them opens with a promise. Every one of them makes the listener do a small piece of work — decide which type they are, admit they're in a slow month — before anything is sold.

The structural difference from Iles

They demo. He promises.

{{AUTO_N}} of {{N_TX}} transcripts here are screen-recorded product walkthroughs: a funnel being built, an AI voice agent answering a live call, an Angi or Yelp integration firing. Daniel Iles has 2,116 ads in his library and not one does this.

He carries belief with a guarantee — "one million views or you don't pay." This cohort carries it by showing the machine working. Exactly one ad across all {{N_ADS}} uses a work-for-free promise, and it is 55 days old.

Both approaches sell to the same buyer. Only one of them has an opener family with a 7-day median.

What this can't tell you

  • No spend or conversion data exists for commercial ads anywhere in Meta's library. Tenure means "not switched off", never "profitable".
  • One snapshot. No kill rates, no stop dates, no trend. Re-pull in two weeks and the deltas become real — that is when this gets genuinely useful.
  • Nine advertisers found by four text queries is not the whole market. There is no working niche filter to enumerate it properly.
  • The top hook rows rest on 2–4 ads. Treat them as leads to test, not conclusions.
  • Four ads are in Malay (Authentic Advisory Systems, Malaysian market). Their 300-day median is real; the market isn't yours.
  • {{N_TX}} of {{N_ADS}} ads have transcripts. The rest are static or have no audio on file.
  • ASR garbles terms — "vet spa" and "MedSpar" are both med spa. Read for structure, not wording.

If you take four things

What to do

  • Drop the guarantee from your opener. Lowest median here, zero survivors in Iles's niche cohort. Two corpora agree. Keep it as a closing line if you want it at all.
  • Open by denying the obvious. "Your lead problem isn't what you think" — 147 days. "You don't have to run discounts" — 121 days. Both refuse the expected pitch before making one.
  • Demo something. Show the machine working. It buys belief more cheaply than a promise does, and it's the one format Iles's entire library is missing.
  • Watch mrmedspa.com and authenticadvisorysystems.com. Small books, high medians — they look like they found a winner and stopped testing. Those are the ads to study.
