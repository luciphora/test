Method

How these ads were picked

Meta's Ad Library publishes no spend, impressions or conversions for commercial ads. Two signals are available, and both are inference:

  • Longevity. A performance advertiser does not pay to keep a losing ad live. The longer an ad runs, the more of his own culling it has survived.
  • Where new money goes. Fresh ads pushed into a segment mean the last round earned another look; a segment skipped in a new wave is one he's stopped believing in.

Everything featured here is ranked on those two. Nothing was chosen because it read well.

Limits — read before quoting any of this

  • Longevity is a proxy, not profit. A well-funded advertiser can keep a mediocre ad alive. Longevity says "not killed", not "profitable".
  • Most stopped ads have no usable tenure. 98% of the historical inactive ads report exactly one day, a data artifact. The only dead ads with a real tenure are the {{N_DIED}} that died between the two snapshots — and those are the ones this report leans on.
  • A week is one data point. The retreat reading rests on wave 2 dying, the 7-week cull, and wave 3 skipping the niches. It's consistent, but a fourth wave into medical would change it. Re-pull in two weeks.
  • Transcripts are machine ASR and garble brand terms — "MedSpot" for med spa, "Rufus" for roofers. Quotes are directionally right, not verbatim.
  • Coverage: every ad live on either snapshot regardless of age, plus every ad started since 6 June 2026. Inactive ads older than that are outside this pull.

If you take four things

What to do with it

  • Trades are a confirmed no. Killed by week seven, never re-funded, untouched by three waves. That verdict survived the week intact.
  • Medical works but isn't a free lunch. He kept about half at eight weeks and didn't scale it. If you go there, expect to cull hard, not to coast.
  • Name the prospect's status quo; don't lead with the guarantee in a niche. Status-quo and authority hooks hold at 33–40%; the guarantee is at 7% inside the niches and 400+ days outside them.
  • Copy his cadence. 48-hour kill on new batches, a hard review at seven weeks, one script re-shot per vertical. The production cost of testing a vertical is one afternoon.
