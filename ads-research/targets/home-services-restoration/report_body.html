Read this first: what the data cannot tell you

Meta publishes no spend, impressions or conversions for commercial ads. Two signals survive: how long an ad has stayed live, and where the advertiser is putting new money. Longevity means an ad was not killed — never that it was profitable.

Dead-ad tenure is censored. Foreplay reports roughly one day for most inactive ads regardless of the truth. So every day-figure here comes from live ads only; dead ads count toward totals and nothing else. One snapshot: a state, not a trend.

Your own ads are not in this. Certified Safe is not indexed by Foreplay — a domain lookup and a name search both returned nothing — so this says what the category does, not what you are doing differently. Send a Meta Ad Library link for your page and the comparison becomes direct.

Local Boston rivals are effectively invisible here. A search for Boston restoration advertisers returned a seed company, a dog bakery and hair-restoration clinics. The advertisers below are national or out-of-state operators running the same plays at larger scale. Treat them as the playbook, not as the people bidding against you on Route 1.

The offer, dismantled

Unlike most local-service categories, the biggest number in a duct or carpet ad is the price — not a saving, not a bonus value. Classifying every dollar figure by the words in front of it:

$99 is the number the category argues with. {{N_ANTI}} of its mentions are not quotes at all — they are premium operators naming the coupon price as the thing they are not: "real service — not a $99 cheap sweep", "this is not a $99 blow-and-go clean". That is the opening for a licensed operator with upfront pricing: you do not have to beat $99, you have to make it the wrong question.

The ladder itself is tight and public: $99 / $119 / $129 / $149 / $159 / $199 / $249. Those are entry prices for a first job, usually gated — {{N_FIRST}} ads say "first-time customers only". The job of the number is to buy the appointment, not the margin.

What the body copy carries that the voiceover doesn't

Three of these transfer directly to Certified Safe:

  • Guarantee ({{N_GUAR}} ads) — "the most thorough cleaning or it's free" carries the five longest-running live ads in the corpus. It is doing the work that a discount usually does, without cutting the price.
  • Cost reframe ({{N_COST}} ads) — the strongest duct angle is not health, it is the energy bill. "Clogged, dusty vents aren't just making you sneeze — they're costing you money." Your site leads on 99.7% particulate reduction; the market leads on the bill.
  • Replace vs restore ({{N_REPL}} ads) — "before you replace, let us reset" anchors a $149 clean against a four-figure replacement. It is the only move in the corpus that raises perceived value instead of lowering price.

Referral partner ({{N_REF}} ads) is the restoration answer. 1-800 WATER DAMAGE runs almost no consumer ads — it advertises to plumbers, property managers and insurance adjusters, asking for the referral rather than the job. For mold and water damage in Boston that is a far more plausible use of Meta than chasing homeowners mid-emergency.

What I'd do with this

  1. Split the campaign by buying mode, not by service list. Duct, carpet and dryer vent are one campaign with a gated entry price and a booking page. Mold and water damage are not a Meta prospecting problem and should stop competing for that budget.
  2. Build the booking page. {{PCT_DC_BOOK}}% of the segment that works lands on a quote or scheduling page; {{PCT_R_BOOK}}% of the segment that doesn't. That is the single clearest structural difference in the whole scan.
  3. Lead ducts on the energy bill, not the particulate count. {{N_COST}} ads make the cost argument and the deepest live duct ads are among them.
  4. Put a guarantee where your discount is. {{N_GUAR}} ads carry one, including every one of the five longest-running live ads in the corpus.
  5. For mold and water damage, advertise to referrers. {{N_REF}} ads already do — plumbers, property managers, adjusters. In a Boston radius that is a countable, reachable audience; panicking homeowners are not.
